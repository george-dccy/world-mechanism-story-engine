# Counterfactual Protocol

Counterfactuals are diagnostic instruments, not decoration.

## Operations

### Remove
Delete an ability, signal, institution, resource, or shortcut.

### Expose
Make an invisible cost, rule, dependency, or signal visible.

### Amplify
Increase a weak effect until adaptation becomes obvious.

### Delay / Accelerate
Change the time between action and consequence.

### Localize
Make a rule apply only to one place, group, relationship, or layer.

### Invert
Swap who owns information, pays cost, receives benefit, or makes the decision.

## Consequence ladder

For each useful counterfactual:
1. **First order** — What does a person do differently today?
2. **Second order** — How do others adapt to that behavior?
3. **Third order** — What institution, relationship, market, norm, or technology changes because adaptation has become normal?

## Strength test

A strong rule:
- creates at least three different behaviors without adding new magic;
- changes meaning over repetition;
- affects both beneficiaries and cost-bearers;
- produces adaptation, not just disaster.

A weak rule:
- generates one visual gag;
- requires new exceptions every scene;
- only scales by affecting more people;
- exists only to deliver a predetermined moral.
