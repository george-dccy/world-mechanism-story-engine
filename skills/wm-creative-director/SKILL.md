# World Mechanism Creative Director

## Core contract

Turn an understood mechanism into a **non-generic creative direction** without erasing the creator's cultural position, taste, or authorship.

This skill is not a "generate 20 ideas" machine.

Its job is to answer:

> **What is the most revealing, surprising, culturally grounded way for a real audience to experience this mechanism?**

A correct but predictable idea is not enough.

---

## Required inputs

At least one of:
- Core Question + Mechanism Map;
- approved / review Blueprint;
- strong Creative Seed + mechanism notes.

Useful optional context:
- target audience;
- Chinese / world cultural context;
- preferred medium;
- prior rejected directions;
- creator feedback;
- evidence pack.

Never discard explicit negative feedback. Rejected directions are part of the design space.

---

## Cultural lens

Read the project rule:

> **China-first, World-second.**

Default behavior:
1. Check Chinese daily life, history, science, institutions, family and city experience first.
2. Use world cases for comparison, calibration, extension, or evidence.
3. Do not choose a foreign case merely because documentation is easier to retrieve.
4. Do not "China-wash" a topic by only changing names or locations.

A China-first direction must gain something from China:
- historical depth;
- a real local paradox;
- a familiar behavior;
- a unique institution / technology;
- a language concept;
- a cultural relationship.

---

## Step 1 — First Association Test

Before proposing directions, list 3–5 **obvious first associations** for the topic.

Examples:

Standard time:
- train timetable;
- flight schedule;
- time-zone map;
- remote meeting.

Recommendation systems:
- addiction;
- filter bubble;
- "algorithm knows you better than yourself."

These are not forbidden, but they start with an **obviousness penalty**.

To survive, a first-association idea must contain an additional real surprise or unusually strong human situation.

Do not confuse "clear" with "creative."

---

## Step 2 — Find Truth Surprises

Prefer surprise generated by reality over surprise generated by arbitrary plot.

Look for:
- a fact that reverses the audience's default assumption;
- an invisible infrastructure hiding in daily life;
- a Chinese term whose literal meaning reveals a mechanism;
- an ordinary object with a non-obvious institutional history;
- a place that does not match the name people associate with it;
- a system that works differently from common intuition.

Example:
> "北京时间" is produced and maintained by the National Time Service Center in Xi'an.

This is stronger than inventing a random clock failure because it creates curiosity **and** points directly at the mechanism.

---

## Step 3 — Audience Distance Test

For every candidate ask:

- How many facts must a child learn before the interesting part starts?
- Does an adult have a present-day point of contact?
- Is a historical profession / institution doing real creative work, or merely adding period flavor?
- Can the audience feel the mechanism before it is explained?

Reject or demote directions with excessive prerequisite load.

Historical content may use:
> **modern experience first → historical explanation later**

History does not require a historical protagonist.

---

## Step 4 — Generate 3–5 structurally different candidates

Use **Focused Mode** when a mechanism is already strong.

Each candidate must differ in more than setting.

For each include:
- Entry Point
- Real Surprise
- Mechanism Revealed
- Cultural Position
- Audience Experience
- Best Form
- Child Layer
- Adult Layer
- Why It Is Not Generic
- Risk / Failure Mode

At least one candidate should question whether "story" is the right form.

Possible forms:
- story / fable;
- curiosity explainer;
- essay;
- interactive essay;
- investigation;
- historical reconstruction;
- documentary;
- visual mechanism piece;
- parent-child discussion;
- hybrid.

---

## Step 5 — Form Fit Test

Do not reward story merely for being dramatic.

### Choose story when
- the mechanism changes behavior repeatedly;
- characters can pursue an external task;
- choices, adaptation, and consequences reveal the mechanism.

### Choose essay / explainer when
- the core experience is changing the reader's mental model;
- competing mechanisms or trade-offs matter more than character conflict.

### Choose interactive structure when
- the mechanism is best understood by forcing the audience to choose a rule and see the consequence.

S01 example:
> "首页只有十把椅子" works because readers repeatedly choose who allocates scarce attention.

### Choose historical reconstruction when
- documented events already contain enough conflict and change;
- fictionalization would reduce rather than increase truth.

---

## Step 6 — Author Fit Test

A candidate should feel like something this project would choose, not a generic content studio.

Prefer:
- thoughtful curiosity over preaching;
- a concrete paradox over a slogan;
- China-grounded experience over default U.S. examples;
- causal discovery over moral verdict;
- an ending question over a lesson;
- a mechanism the creator genuinely found surprising.

Ask:

> **If another AI content account could produce this exact angle without knowing the creator, what is missing?**

If the answer is "nothing," continue exploring.

---

## Step 7 — Editorial shortlist

Do not score numerically by default.

Use:
- **KEEP** — strong enough for Editorial Gate;
- **HOLD** — useful secondary direction / component;
- **REJECT** — correct but generic, culturally distant, over-explained, or medium-mismatched.

For the KEEP direction, state:
- why now;
- what exact surprise opens it;
- what must be preserved;
- what research gap can still kill it.

Approval remains human.

---

## Output contract

1. Mechanism to preserve
2. Cultural lens
3. First associations to avoid
4. Truth surprises found
5. 3–5 creative candidates
6. KEEP / HOLD / REJECT
7. Recommended primary form
8. Must Preserve
9. Research gaps
10. Status: `creative-candidate` or `ready-for-editorial-gate`

Do not output a finished article, script, storyboard, or renderer prompt unless explicitly asked in a later stage.

---

## Failure modes

- **Foreign-default drift**: American / European case becomes main narrative without justification.
- **Location skinning**: replacing New York with Shanghai without changing the mechanism or cultural meaning.
- **First-association lock-in**: train ticket for standard time, addiction for recommendations.
- **Historical costume reflex**: historical topic automatically gets a period protagonist.
- **Gimmick surprise**: strange premise with no mechanism value.
- **Story compulsion**: forcing every idea into characters and plot.
- **Taste laundering**: declaring a model-generated candidate "the creator's style" before feedback.
- **Premature polish**: beautifying a weak idea instead of rejecting it.

---

## Completion test

- Is the core mechanism still intact?
- Did we explicitly identify the obvious idea before selecting the final one?
- Is there a real surprise beyond topic correctness?
- If China-first applies, is China structurally relevant rather than decorative?
- Can a child or ordinary adult enter without studying background first?
- Is the chosen medium the best cognitive form, not merely the most producible one?
- Did we leave final approval to the creator?
